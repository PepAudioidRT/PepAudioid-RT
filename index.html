Give your hardware a second life

Old Hardware. Real-Time Audio.

Your old PC might still have a lot of music left in it. PepAudioid RT explores how older desktops and laptops can become dedicated digital audio players, using a lightweight Linux environment, MPD, ALSA and a USB DAC.

▣

Intel Core i3-M350 · 2010

An older laptop successfully tested with the PepAudioid RT Live USB, PREEMPT_RT kernel and FLAC playback through an external USB DAC.

65 µs maximum observed series-wide worst-case scheduling latency in the recorded Live test.

▣

HP 280 G2 · Intel Core i3-6100

Our development and audio-testing platform. A modest desktop running a custom PREEMPT_RT kernel, MPD and ALSA with a Musical Fidelity V90-DAC.

31 µs highest observed series-wide worst-case scheduling latency across three recorded RT test series.

No expensive modern CPU is required for basic lossless audio playback. Actual compatibility and real-time performance depend on the hardware. Low scheduling latency does not, by itself, demonstrate superior audible sound quality.

Measured, not marketed

Real-world latency tests

Selected observations from our own test sessions. These are scheduling-latency results, not measurements of sound quality or proof of audible superiority.

16 µsHP 280 G2 · lowest series-wide worst case in one 10 × 2-minute cyclictest series (RT kernel)
31 µsHP 280 G2 · highest observed worst case across three recorded RT test series
65 µsIntel i3-M350 · highest observed worst case in a 10 × 2-minute Live USB test series

Hardware: HP 280 G2 / i3-6100 and Intel i3-M350. Tests used kernel 7.3.1-necroaudio3-rt1 and cyclictest; configurations and load conditions varied by series. The HP figures are from the 5 Nov 2026 test series; the i3-M350 figure is from 12 Nov 2026. An XRUN monitor reported zero observed episodes in a separate session, but its audio-XRUN sensitivity was not validated by an induced event. Full protocols and reproducibility materials are planned.

Get started

How to try PepAudioid RT

Live USB workflow

  1. Check the Releases page for an available ISO.
  2. Verify the published SHA-256 checksum before use.
  3. Write the ISO to a spare USB drive using a suitable imaging tool.
  4. Boot compatible hardware and follow the release-specific instructions.

Writing an image erases the selected USB drive. Back up anything important first.

Project status

PepAudioid RT is an experimental, evolving project. The next ISO version is still under development. Download availability, hardware compatibility, and exact setup steps will be documented per release rather than assumed.

Want to help? Share your hardware, DAC model, scheduling-latency results and playback observations through GitHub Issues.
